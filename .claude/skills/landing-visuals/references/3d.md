# 3D

## CSS 3D first

Everything we need so far (tilted screenshots, stacked layers, flip cards, a carousel, a camera flying
past real screens) is CSS 3D: `perspective` on a parent, `transform-style: preserve-3d`, `translateZ`,
`rotateX/Y`, `backface-visibility: hidden`. It costs bytes in the hundreds, uses the compositor, keeps
real text and images in the DOM (selectable, accessible, crisp) and needs no WebGL fallback.

Rules: one `perspective` per scene (800-1200px reads natural); keep rotations small (under 20 degrees
for tilt, a flip is 180); `preserve-3d` breaks under `overflow: hidden`, `filter` or `opacity < 1` on the
same element, so put those on a wrapper; text on a rotated plane gets blurry, keep the resting pose flat.

## Scroll camera, cheaply

`components/scroll-camera.js` (+ `.css`): a tall section with a sticky stage, panes placed with
`--x --y --z --ry`, and scroll progress moving the whole world opposite to a camera that eases between
stops. About 1.3 KB gzip. Panes not near the camera fade to 35%. With reduced motion or no JS it is a plain
column of figures. Keep 3-5 stops; each stop shows one real screen and the copy beside it says one thing.

The scroll-world approach (pre-rendered video of a flight through generated scenes, scrubbed by scroll)
gives a richer result but needs a video generator, megabytes of media and invented imagery. We do not
use generated worlds; the honest version for us is scrubbing a real app recording (see motion.md).

## When three.js would be worth it

Only for something CSS cannot fake: real lighting on a 3D model, thousands of particles, a shader effect.
Cost: three.js core is MIT but about 150 KB+ gzip even tree-shaken, far over our 15 KB page budget,
plus a canvas that is invisible to screen readers and needs a static fallback. If ever justified, it
must be vendored into `site/assets/vendor/` with its LICENSE, loaded only on the page that needs it, lazy
(after the hero paints), paused off screen, `dpr` capped at 2, and the skill must record why in BRAND.md.
Habits worth keeping from the three.js skills even in canvas 2D: cap device pixel ratio, render on demand
instead of every frame, dispose of what you create, size from the container not the window.

## GSAP

Not used. Its timeline and ScrollTrigger ideas (scrub tied to scroll, pinning, one timeline per scene,
labels) are what our small scripts do by hand. GSAP's library licence is GreenSock's own standard
licence, not MIT (the gsap-skills repo text is MIT, the library is not), and adds 25 KB+ gzip; adopting it
needs a licence check and a budget exception.
